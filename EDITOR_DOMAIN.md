# Domain: South Asian studies for high school readers

## What the manuscript is
*Roots & Rivers*, a 22-chapter MkDocs textbook (docs/chapters/NN-slug/index.md, quiz.md, references.md) teaching South Asian geography, history, faiths, languages, festivals, food, arts, family life, the eight countries, and the diaspora. The one question the book answers: "Where do the traditions in South Asian families come from, and what do they mean?"

## The reader (this replaces the skill's default reader)
A 14-17-year-old American high schooler. Some are South Asian heritage kids who know practices but not reasons; some have no background at all. Two consequences: never assume knowledge of any language, faith or history; and never trade truth for simplicity. A simplification must still be true at the level stated and say where it stops working ("many Hindu families", "in most of Bangladesh"). A false simplification or an overgeneralization about a people is a blocking defect.

## The five-part standard, adapted for this book
1. A precise statement (who, where, when, with a date or era).
2. Every term defined, with an example (and original script plus respelling where relevant).
3. The practice or idea shown in its smallest real case (one festival, one dish, one family).
4. The limit or caveat: where it varies by region, faith, class, caste, gender or generation; what scholars debate.
5. A tie to something concrete: the MicroSim, Zara and Ben, a sourced number, a UNESCO listing, a primary source.

## Ideas that must never be blurred
- Region vs nation vs language vs script vs religion vs ethnicity (e.g. Punjabi is a language and a regional identity spanning India and Pakistan; Bengali is not a religion; "Hindi" is not "Indian").
- Partition (1947) vs the independence of Bangladesh (1971).
- Arranged vs forced marriage.
- Varna (a textual four-fold scheme) vs jati (thousands of endogamous groups in practice); caste vs class.
- Migration vs invasion in ancient history; scholarly consensus vs minority or political positions.
- A tradition's history vs a medical claim (Ayurveda, yoga).
- UNESCO World Heritage (places) vs UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (practices).
- "Official language" vs "national language" (India has no national language).
- Fictional characters (Zara, Ben and friends) vs real historical people.
- Country-level statistics vs community-level generalizations.

## Canonical values
See the "Canonical values" section of CLAUDE.md. The prose must match those values everywhere.

## Currency rule
Every statistic, law, leader, population, record and "latest" claim must be the most recent available as of October 2026, with its year in the prose. Check primary sources. Anything about current governments (Afghanistan after 2021, Bangladesh after 2024, Sri Lanka after 2022, Nepal) is stated briefly, neutrally and with a date.

## Sensitivity standard
Read every chapter as a member of each community it describes would. Flag: exoticizing words, one-country bias, a faith described from outside as myth, a disputed territory stated as belonging to one side, numbers of victims given without a range or source, caste or gender stereotypes, and anything a grandparent from that community would call wrong or unfair.
