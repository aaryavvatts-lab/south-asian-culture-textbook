# Pilot Study Kit: Does Learning With *Roots & Rivers* Help?

This folder is a ready-to-run kit for a small, minimal-risk pilot study. It asks two questions:

1. After students use Chapters 1 to 8 of *Roots & Rivers*, do their scores on a short knowledge test go up?
2. Do students feel more confident explaining South Asian traditions, and do they talk with family or community members about them more often?

The kit is meant for a student researcher, a teacher or a club advisor. It is **not** published on the book's website.

## Stop: ethics and approval come first

**Do not recruit anyone, hand out any form, or collect any data until the study is approved in writing.** This study involves minors, so it needs:

1. **Permission from your school.** Your advisor and a school administrator must agree that the study can happen in the club or class, and when.
2. **Review by a committee.** If the study will go to a science fair, it must be approved by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) **before** any recruitment or data collection. Society for Science's ISEF rules say a school IRB needs at least three members: an educator who is not the Adult Sponsor, a school administrator, and a medical or mental health professional. Human participant projects use ISEF Form 4. See the [ISEF Human Participants rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/). If your project is not for a fair, still get your school's written approval using the same materials.
3. **Written parent or guardian permission** (`consent-parent.md`) **and student assent** (`assent-student.md`) for every participant under 18. ISEF rules also say that when a study with minors includes a survey, the survey must be attached to the parent permission form, so attach `survey.md` (and `pre-post-test.md`) when you send the permission form home.

## The ethics rules for this study

- **Voluntary.** Students can say no, skip any question, or stop at any time, with no effect on grades, club membership or anything else. Club meetings and lessons go on the same way for everyone; only the test and survey are optional.
- **No names.** Tests and surveys use a code (for example, `P07`), never a name. The list linking names to codes is kept by the advisor, on paper, in a locked place, and destroyed after the post-test is matched. The researcher sees only codes.
- **No family history required.** No question asks about a student's ethnicity, religion, caste, immigration story or family. The survey asks only whether a student talked with someone about traditions, not what was said. Students without South Asian heritage take part in exactly the same way.
- **Group results only.** Results are reported as totals and averages for the whole group. Never report a result for a group so small that someone could be identified (for example, "the two seniors").
- **Not graded.** The pre-test and post-test never count toward any grade.
- **Conflicts of interest.** If the researcher helped write the book, say so in the report and to the review committee. Ask someone else to score the tests with the answer key.

## Protocol

**Design:** one group, pre-test and post-test, no comparison group.

**Participants:** high school students in a club or class (for example, a South Asian Student Association) who return both signed forms.

**Instruction:** Chapters 1 to 8 and their MicroSims. In a club that meets monthly for 45 minutes, this takes most of a school year (see the [Club Kit](../../docs/club-kit.md)). In a class, it takes about two to three weeks.

**Steps:**

1. Read every file in this folder. Fill in every `[bracket]`.
2. Get your advisor's agreement and your school's written permission.
3. If needed, submit to your IRB with this README, the test, the survey and both forms. Make any changes they ask for. Keep the signed approval.
4. Send home `consent-parent.md` with the test and survey attached. Collect it, then collect `assent-student.md` from students.
5. The advisor assigns codes (`P01`, `P02` and so on) and keeps the only key.
6. **Pre-test session:** the 20-question test (about 15 minutes) and the survey (about 5 minutes), before Chapter 1.
7. **Instruction:** teach the chapters as usual. Do not teach to the test questions, and don't go over the test with students.
8. **Post-test session:** the same test and the same survey, after Chapter 8.
9. Enter the data into `data-template.csv` using codes only. Have a second person check five random tests.
10. Destroy the name-to-code key once the pre and post tests are matched.
11. Analyze with `analysis-guide.md`.
12. Share group results with participants and the school. You may report group totals (never raw data) through the book's [classroom use form](https://github.com/aaryavvatts-lab/south-asian-culture-textbook/issues/new?template=2-classroom-use.yml).

**Data storage:** paper tests in a locked drawer or cabinet controlled by the advisor; the spreadsheet in a school account, shared only with the advisor and researcher. Delete or shred everything by [date, for example one year after the study ends].

## What a pilot can and can't show

A pilot is a small first try. It can show whether scores went up and how students felt. It **can't** prove the book caused the change. There is no comparison group, the group is small, students chose to join, and they may have learned from family, other classes or the news in the same months. `analysis-guide.md` explains how to say this clearly.
