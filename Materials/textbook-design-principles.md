# Design Principles: what makes Roots & Rivers different

## The core argument

Second-generation South Asian teens in the United States often grow up surrounded by traditions they were never taught to explain. Pew Research Center reports that 68% of US-born Asian Americans speak only English at home, compared with 14% of Asian immigrants ([Pew, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-indians-in-the-u-s/)). Their friends are curious and have nowhere good to start. Minnesota now requires high schools to offer an ethnic studies course beginning in 2026-27 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/education/minnesota-social-studies-standards-add-ethnic-studies); [Minn. Stat. 120B.021](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/120B.021)), and teachers need free materials.

What exists today, and what it lacks:

- **Dan McCreary's intelligent-textbook gallery** has about 150 interactive books. None is about South Asia ([gallery](https://dmccreary.github.io/intelligent-textbooks/case-studies/)).
- **CK-12 / LibreTexts K12 "South Asia" unit:** short, text-only, written for a Texas geography course, and uses outdated framing and terms.
- **Khan Academy:** strong on ancient history and art history, but nothing on festivals, food, languages, family life, Partition or the diaspora.
- **University outreach centers** (UT Austin South Asia Institute, UW-Madison Center for South Asia): excellent, but written for teachers, mostly static downloads, often one region or theme.
- **SAADA:** a superb diaspora archive, but it covers the diaspora only and is aimed at college users.
- **Nothing** is student-facing, interactive, free, current, covering all eight countries, and designed to send teens back to their own families with good questions.

## Dimensions (Problem → Solution)

1. **"I don't know where to start." → A path.** 22 chapters in four parts, each with a short summary, prerequisites and a learning graph so nothing is used before it's taught.
2. **"It's just a list of facts." → Interactivity in every chapter.** One flagship MicroSim per chapter that teaches one idea by doing: spin the festival calendar, clap a tala, draw a kolam, build a thali, trace a script family.

3. **"Culture lives in people, not books." → Ask Your Elders.** Every chapter ends its teaching with a short interview guide that sends the reader to a parent, grandparent or community elder. Chapter 22 turns the 21 interviews into a real oral history project, with consent and a community story archive.
4. **"Whose version is this?" → Whose Story? boxes.** Every chapter shows how different communities, countries or historians tell the same thing differently. Disputed questions are presented as disputed.
5. **"India-only books."** → **All eight countries.** Every theme chapter has named examples from at least four countries, and four chapters tour all eight countries directly.
6. **Practice and feedback.** A 10-question quiz per chapter, 5 Check Yourself questions, Myth/Reality pairs and 12 Bloom-level exercises.
7. **Accuracy you can check.** Every number and date is sourced and linked, with a public corrections log, and fact-checking by independent reviewers.
8. **Free and open forever.** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, no login, works on a phone, printable PDFs, a teacher guide and a club kit.

## The design contract (every chapter, no exceptions)

- [ ] Summary, Concepts Covered (exact labels), Prerequisites, mascot welcome
- [ ] Zara and Ben hook (N.1) ending in a question
- [ ] Intuition before detail; every concept bolded at first definition
- [ ] Key terms in original script + respelling + meaning (verified)
- [ ] A Whose Story? box
- [ ] One MicroSim, schematic maps only, no console errors
- [ ] Ask Your Elders, exact template and closing line
- [ ] 3-5 Myth/Reality pairs
- [ ] Check Yourself (5), Exercises (12, two per Bloom level), Key Takeaways (5-7)
- [ ] Named examples from at least 4 of the 8 countries (theme chapters)
- [ ] Every statistic and date sourced; no invented quotes; sensitivity rules followed
- [ ] At most 6 mascot boxes, never back-to-back

## The test question

"Would a 14-year-old with no South Asian background understand this, and would a South Asian grandparent reading over their shoulder nod and say 'yes, that's right, and that's fair'?" If either answer is no, rewrite it.
