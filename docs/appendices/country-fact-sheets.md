# Appendix B: Country Fact Sheets

This appendix gives a one-table snapshot of each of the eight countries of South Asia, listed alphabetically. Use it for quick reference while you read, or as a starting point for a club presentation. The country chapters ([17](../chapters/17-india/index.md) to [20](../chapters/20-nepal-bhutan-maldives/index.md)) tell the fuller stories.

**How to read these tables**

- **Official name** is the formal name used by the United Nations (UNTERM country list, checked October 2026).
- **Population** is the 2025 figure from the World Bank (SP.POP.TOTL, dataset updated October 2026).
- **Main faiths** are Pew Research Center estimates for 2020, published in June 2025. We use one source for all eight countries so they can be compared fairly. National census figures, where the book uses them, appear in the country chapters, and they can differ a little from Pew's estimates. Percentages are rounded and may not add to 100.
- **Currency** names and codes come from the international ISO 4217 list (September 2026 edition).
- **UNESCO World Heritage sites** are counted from each country's page on the UNESCO World Heritage Centre website, checked in October 2026. A site shared between countries counts for each country that lists it.

!!! mascot-tip "Numbers have birthdays"
    ![Mora giving a tip](../img/mascot/tip.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    Every number on this page has a year attached, because populations, faith shares and even site lists change. If you use one in a project, copy the year and the source along with it.

## Afghanistan

| | |
|---|---|
| Official name | The Islamic Republic of Afghanistan (UN name). Since August 2021 the country has been governed by the Taliban, who call the state the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan. | Capital | Kabul |
| Population (2025, World Bank) | 43,844,111 (about 44 million) |
| Official languages | Pashto and Dari |
| Main faiths (Pew, 2020) | Muslim 99.9%; most are Sunni, with a Shia minority |
| Currency | Afghani (AFN) |
| National day | Independence Day, 19 August. Afghanistan was never formally colonized, but Britain controlled its foreign affairs until the 1919 Treaty of Rawalpindi. |
| UNESCO World Heritage sites | 2: Minaret and Archaeological Remains of Jam (2002); Cultural Landscape and Archaeological Remains of the Bamiyan Valley (2003) |

| | |
|---|---|
| Official name | The Kingdom of Bhutan |
| Capital | Thimphu |
| Population (2025, World Bank) | 796,682 (about 800,000) |
| National language | Dzongkha |

## India

| | |
|---|---|
| Official name | The Republic of India (also Bharat in the Constitution's own words) |
| Capital | New Delhi |
| Population (2025, World Bank) | 1,463,865,525 (about 1.46 billion) |
| Official languages | Hindi and English for the national government; the Constitution's Eighth Schedule lists 22 languages |
| Main faiths (Pew, 2020) | Hindu 79.4%, Muslim 15.2%, Christian 2.2%, other faiths (including Sikhs and Jains) 2.5% together; Buddhists under 1% |
| Currency | Indian rupee (INR) |
| Independence | 15 August 1947 (Independence Day); the Constitution took effect on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day) |
| UNESCO World Heritage sites | 45 (37 cultural, 7 natural, 1 mixed). Examples: Taj Mahal (1983); Western Ghats (2012); Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath (2026), the newest. |
| National symbol | The peacock has been the national bird since 1963. |
| Disputed territory | Kashmir is disputed. India, Pakistan and China each administer part of the region, and India and Pakistan each claim the whole former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. India also claims Aksai Chin, which China administers. |
