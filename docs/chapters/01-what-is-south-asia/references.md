# References: What Is South Asia?

1. [Population, total (SP.POP.TOTL)](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL) - World Bank Open Data - The source for every 2025 population number in this chapter. You can look up any of the eight countries yourself and see how their populations have changed since 1960.

2. [World Bank Country and Lending Groups](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/906519-world-bank-country-and-lending-groups) - World Bank Data Help Desk - Lists which countries the World Bank puts in each region. It shows why the World Bank's "South Asia" total is smaller than the eight-country total: Afghanistan and Pakistan are now in its "Middle East, North Africa, Afghanistan and Pakistan" group.

3. [Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) on Monsoon](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf) - India Meteorological Department - The official explanation of how monsoon rainfall is measured, what "normal" means, active and break spells, and the normal dates when the monsoon reaches Kerala (around 1 June) and covers India (around 8 July).

4. [Winds Connect Snow to Sea](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/winds-connect-snow-to-sea) - NASA Earth Observatory (Rebecca Lindsey) - A clear, illustrated explanation of how uneven heating of land and ocean drives the Asian monsoon, and how snow cover can affect its strength.

5. [Monsoon](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/monsoon/) - National Geographic Education - A short encyclopedia entry on what a monsoon is, how the South Asian summer and winter monsoons differ, and why farmers depend on the summer rains.

6. [The Himalayas](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/himalaya.html) - US Geological Survey, *This Dynamic Earth* - Explains how the subcontinent's plate drifted north and collided with Asia about 40 to 50 million years ago, and why the Himalaya is still rising.

7. [Eighth Schedule: Languages](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/schedules/languages) - Constitution of India (constitutionofindia.net) - The full list of the 22 languages recognized in India's Constitution, with a note on how the list grew from 14 in 1949.

8. [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) - Wikipedia - A useful starting point on the history of the terms "Indian subcontinent" and "South Asia," with links to the scholarly sources behind the naming debate.

9. [Monsoon of South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_of_South_Asia) - Wikipedia - An overview of how the southwest and northeast monsoons work, the role of the Western Ghats and Himalaya, and the monsoon's effects on farming. Use it to find primary sources such as IMD reports.

10. [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation) - Wikipedia - Background on SAARC's founding in Dhaka in 1985, Afghanistan's entry in 2007, its secretariat in Kathmandu, and its summits. Follow its citations to official SAARC documents.
