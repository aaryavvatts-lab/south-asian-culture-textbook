# References: Ancient Roots

1. [Archaeological Ruins at Moenjodaro](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/138/) - UNESCO World Heritage Centre - The official World Heritage listing (1980) for Mohenjo-daro in Sindh, Pakistan. It describes the grid streets, baked-brick buildings, drainage and citadel, and explains how flooding and salt threaten the ruins today.

2. [Indus civilization](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Indus-civilization) - Encyclopaedia Britannica - A detailed, regularly updated overview of the Indus cities: their dates (about 2600 to 1900 BCE), how far they spread, their crops, seals, weights and script, and the debate over how they declined.

3. [Officials Are Offering $1 Million to Anyone Who Can Decode This Ancient Script](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/officials-are-offering-1-million-to-anyone-who-can-decode-this-ancient-script-180985922/) - Smithsonian Magazine (January 2025) - A short news story on Tamil Nadu's 2025 prize for deciphering the Indus Script and why the puzzle is so hard. It shows why this ancient question still makes headlines.

4. [The formation of human populations in South and Central Asia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31488661/) - Narasimhan and colleagues, *Science* (2019), PubMed record - The large ancient DNA study of 523 people behind the chapter's discussion of the Indo-Aryan Migration. The summary is technical, but the opening paragraph is readable with help from a teacher.

5. [Tradition of Vedic chanting](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tradition-of-vedic-chanting-00062) - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage - Explains the four Vedas and the memory techniques priests use to keep every sound exact, and why UNESCO recognized the tradition (proclaimed 2003, inscribed 2008).

6. [Lumbini, the Birthplace of the Lord Buddha](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/666/) - UNESCO World Heritage Centre - The listing for Lumbini in Nepal, with Ashoka's inscribed pillar and the remains of monasteries and stupas. Note that it gives Nepal's traditional date for the Buddha's birth, which differs from many historians' dates.

7. [Ashoka](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ashoka) - Encyclopaedia Britannica - A clear biography of Ashoka covering the Kalinga war, his turn to Buddhism and dhamma, his rock and pillar edicts, and his support for Buddhist missions to Sri Lanka.

8. [Gupta dynasty](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Gupta-dynasty) - Encyclopaedia Britannica - A short overview of the Gupta kings from Chandragupta I to the Huna invasions, including why historians now question calling the period a "classical" or "golden" age.

9. [Archaeological Site of Nalanda Mahavihara at Nalanda, Bihar](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1502/) - UNESCO World Heritage Centre - The 2016 World Heritage listing for Nalanda, describing its monasteries, temples and art, and its 800 years as a center of Buddhist learning.

10. [Sangam literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangam_literature) - Wikipedia - An overview of the earliest Tamil poetry, the debate over its dates, the akam and puram poems, and the Madurai academy legends. Use it to find the scholarly books it cites, such as Kamil Zvelebil's work.
