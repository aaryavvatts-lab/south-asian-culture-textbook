#### Question 1

In what year was the Delhi Sultanate established?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 1206
2. 1336
3. 1526
4. 1747
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The Delhi Sultanate began in 1206 and lasted until 1526. The year 1526 is when Babur founded the Mughal Empire, which is why it's a tempting distractor. The year 1336 is the founding of Vijayanagara, and 1747 is the founding of the Durrani Empire.

    **Concept Tested:** Delhi Sultanate
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 2

What is the Taj Mahal?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A royal palace where Shah Jahan lived with his family
2. A Sufi shrine built for Moinuddin Chishti
3. A white marble tomb built by order of Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal
4. A fort built by Akbar to guard Agra
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The Taj Mahal is a mausoleum, built in Agra between about 1631 and 1648. Many people think it is a palace because of its size and beauty, but it was built as a tomb, and both Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan are buried there.

    **Concept Tested:** Taj Mahal
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 3

Which teacher, born in 1469 in Talwandi (now Nankana Sahib, Pakistan), do Sikhs honor as the founder of their faith?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Kabir
2. Guru Nanak
3. Nizamuddin Auliya
4. Basavanna
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Guru Nanak (1469 to 1539) is the first of the ten Sikh Gurus. Kabir is a common distractor because some of his verses are in the Sikh scripture, but he was a separate poet-saint of the bhakti tradition.

    **Concept Tested:** Guru Nanak
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 4

Why were the monsoon winds so important to Indian Ocean Trade?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. They blew from the same direction all year, so ships could sail quickly in one direction only.
2. They kept pirates away from the coasts of South Asia.
3. They brought rain that filled the harbors so large ships could enter.
4. They reversed direction with the seasons, so sailors could travel out on one wind and return on the other.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The monsoon winds blow from the southwest for part of the year and from the northeast for the rest, which let ships make round trips across the ocean. Option A gets the key idea backwards: it's the reversal that made return voyages possible.

    **Concept Tested:** Indian Ocean Trade
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 5

Which statement best describes how Islam first reached South Asia?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Through several routes over centuries, including traders on the coasts, Sufi teachers and conquest in the northwest
2. Only through the Mughal conquest in 1526
3. Only through Arab armies that conquered the whole subcontinent in 711
4. Through European traders who arrived after 1498
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Muslim traders settled on the Kerala and Sri Lankan coasts very early, the Maldives' king converted in the 12th century, Arab forces conquered Sindh around 711 to 712, and Sufi teachers drew many followers. Option C is wrong because the 711 conquest covered Sindh, not the whole subcontinent.

    **Concept Tested:** Early Islam in South Asia
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 6

What does the term Indo-Persian Culture refer to?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The culture of Persian immigrants who lived only in Iran
2. A blend of Persian and South Asian language, art, architecture, food and music that grew in Muslim-ruled courts and cities of South Asia
3. A religion that combined Hinduism and Islam into one faith
4. The European style of buildings brought by the East India Company
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Indo-Persian Culture includes things like the Hindustani language (ancestor of Hindi and Urdu), Mughal architecture and painting, and court cooking. It was a culture shared by people of many faiths, not a new religion, so C is wrong.

    **Concept Tested:** Indo-Persian Culture
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 7

Ayesha shows a photo of the Badshahi Mosque in Lahore, and Arjun shows the Charminar in Hyderabad. Using the chapter, which statement about the two buildings is correct?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Both were built by the Mughal emperor Akbar.
2. Both were built by the Delhi Sultanate.
3. The Charminar was built by the Qutb Shahi sultan of Golconda in 1591, and the Badshahi Mosque by the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb in 1671 to 1673.
4. The Charminar was built by the Vijayanagara Empire, and the Badshahi Mosque by the Durrani Empire.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The two buildings come from two different powers that existed at the same time: the Qutb Shahis ruled Golconda until the Mughals conquered it in 1687. Assuming every Muslim-built monument is Mughal (option A) is exactly the mistake the chapter warns about.

    **Concept Tested:** Mughal Empire
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 8

A classmate says, "In 1600, the British government took over India." Which correction is most accurate?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. In 1600 the Portuguese took over India, not the British.
2. In 1600 the Mughal Empire invited the British government to rule Bengal.
3. In 1600 the Dutch East India Company conquered Sri Lanka.
4. In 1600 the East India Company, a private English trading company, received a royal charter to trade in Asia; it did not rule territory yet.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The East India Company was a private business chartered on 31 December 1600. For more than a century it traded with the permission of Mughal emperors and regional rulers. The Portuguese (option A) held Goa from 1510, but they never took over South Asia as a whole.

    **Concept Tested:** East India Company
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 9

Using the Empire Swim Lanes idea, which pair of powers existed at the same time?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The Chola Dynasty and the Mughal Empire
2. The Vijayanagara Empire and the Bengal Sultanate
3. The Delhi Sultanate and the Maratha Empire
4. The Anuradhapura Kingdom and the Durrani Empire
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Vijayanagara (1336 to 1646) and the Bengal Sultanate (1352 to 1576) overlapped for more than two centuries. The Cholas ended by 1279, long before the Mughals began in 1526, and the Delhi Sultanate ended in 1526, long before Shivaji's coronation in 1674.

    **Concept Tested:** Vijayanagara Empire
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

#### Question 10

What do the Bhakti Movement and Sufism have in common?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Both are branches of Sikhism founded by Guru Nanak.
2. Both rejected music and poetry as distractions from prayer.
3. Both emphasized personal, loving devotion to God, often through poetry and music in local languages, within their own faith traditions.
4. Both were started by the Mughal emperor Akbar to unite his empire.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Bhakti grew within Hinduism and Sufism within Islam, but both focused on devotion and used songs and poetry, such as bhajans and qawwali. They are separate traditions, so they are not branches of Sikhism (A), and both began centuries before Akbar (D).

    **Concept Tested:** Sufism
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze
