# References: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition

1. [Partition of British India](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/teaching-resources/partition-of-british-india/) - The National Archives (UK) - A classroom lesson built from original letters, maps, newspapers and oral history, made with SOAS for the 75th anniversary. It shows how the 3 June plan, the Boundary Commission and the 17 August announcement looked in real documents.

2. [Indian Independence, Part Two](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/teaching-resources/indian-independence-part-two/) - The National Archives (UK) - Primary sources from 1947, including Mountbatten's June broadcast and official reports of refugee movements in Punjab and Delhi. Useful for an Analyze or Evaluate exercise on what officials knew at the time.

3. [The 1947 Partition of South Asia: Introduction](https://guides.library.stanford.edu/c.php?g=1002801) - Stanford University Libraries - A research guide with a short, clear overview of Partition, including the figure of about 14 million refugees, and links to books, films and archives for deeper reading.

4. [The 1947 Partition Archive](https://1947partitionarchive.org/) - The 1947 Partition Archive - A nonprofit that records the life stories of Partition survivors and trains volunteers to interview elders. A model for the Ask Your Elders interviews in this book.

5. [The Big March: Migratory Flows after the Partition of India](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-big-march-migratory-flows-after-the-partition-of-india/) - Bharadwaj, Khwaja and Mian, *Economic and Political Weekly* (2008), via Princeton University - A study that uses census records to estimate about 14.5 million people arriving and 17.9 million leaving, which is the source for the chapter's displacement range.

6. [The South Asia Crisis and the Founding of Bangladesh, 1971](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/south-asia) - Office of the Historian, US Department of State - A short account of the 1970 election, the crackdown, the refugee crisis and the December war, from the point of view of US foreign policy at the time.

7. [Death toll among the Bangladeshi refugees of the 1971 war](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0320760) - Adhikari, Islam and Jalal, *PLOS ONE* (2025) - A recent peer-reviewed study of about 9.9 million refugees who fled to India in 1971. Its introduction shows how widely estimates of the war's total death toll vary, and why.

8. [International Mother Language Day](https://www.unesco.org/en/days/mother-language) - UNESCO - Explains the day that grew out of Bangladesh's Language Movement, approved at UNESCO's 1999 General Conference and observed worldwide since 2000.

9. [Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/articles/article-17-abolition-of-untouchability/) - Constitution of India (constitutionofindia.net) - The text of Article 17 and a summary of how the Constituent Assembly debated and adopted it in 1948, part of B. R. Ambedkar's legacy.

10. [India-Pakistan Conflict in Spring 2025](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13000) - Congressional Research Service (US Congress) - A short, neutral report on the April 2025 Pahalgam attack, the strikes India and Pakistan exchanged in May 2025, and the ceasefire of 10 May. It also gives background on relations between the two countries since 2019.
