#### Question 1

Which school of Buddhism is followed by most Buddhists in Sri Lanka?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Vajrayana
2. Zen
3. Theravada
4. Pure Land
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Sri Lanka follows Theravada Buddhism, whose scriptures are the Pali Canon. Vajrayana is the main distractor: it is the Buddhism of Bhutan and Himalayan Nepal, not Sri Lanka.

    **Concept Tested:** Theravada and Vajrayana
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 2

What is the Guru Granth Sahib?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The Sikh scripture, which Sikhs honor as their living Guru
2. The founder of Sikhism
3. The holiest gurdwara in Amritsar
4. A collection of Hindu epics translated into Punjabi
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The Guru Granth Sahib is the Sikh scripture, first compiled by Guru Arjan in 1604. Guru Nanak founded Sikhism, and the Golden Temple is the holiest gurdwara, which is why B and C can sound plausible.

    **Concept Tested:** Guru Granth Sahib
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 3

What does ahimsa mean?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Devotional singing in a temple
2. Non-violence toward all living beings
3. A pilgrimage to a sacred river
4. Fasting during a holy month
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Ahimsa means non-violence in action, word and thought. Jains put it at the center of their lives, and Hindus and Buddhists also value it. Fasting (D) is part of some Jain festivals, but it is not what the word means.

    **Concept Tested:** Ahimsa
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 4

Which statement best describes Hinduism?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A religion founded by one teacher, with one holy book
2. A branch of Buddhism that began in Nepal
3. A religion that began in Arabia and spread to India
4. A family of related traditions with no single founder and many sacred texts
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Hinduism grew over thousands of years from many traditions and has many sacred texts, including the Vedas, Upanishads, epics and Puranas. Option A describes the common misconception that Hinduism has "one holy book."

    **Concept Tested:** Hinduism
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 5

Why did Sunni and Shia Islam become separate branches?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. They disagreed about who should lead the Muslim community after the Prophet Muhammad died.
2. They use different holy books.
3. Shia Muslims do not follow the Five Pillars.
4. Sunni Islam began in South Asia, while Shia Islam began in Arabia.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Sunnis accept the community's choice of Abu Bakr as the first leader, while Shias believe the Prophet named Ali. Both branches share the Quran and the Five Pillars, so B and C are wrong.

    **Concept Tested:** Sunni and Shia Islam
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 6

What idea does langar, the free community kitchen of a gurdwara, express?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. That only Sikhs may eat food blessed in a gurdwara
2. That fasting is the most important form of worship
3. That all people are equal, so everyone eats together regardless of religion, caste, gender or wealth
4. That food should be offered to the scripture before anyone eats
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Langar is served free to anyone who comes, and everyone sits together as equals, reflecting Guru Nanak's teaching of equality. Option A is the opposite of the truth: visitors of every faith are welcome.

    **Concept Tested:** Langar
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 7

#### Question 8

A classmate writes, "Pakistan is Muslim, India is Hindu, and Sri Lanka is Buddhist, so each faith has its own country." Which fact from the chapter best shows the problem with this claim?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. SAARC was founded in 1985.
2. Bhutan follows Vajrayana Buddhism.
3. The Kumbh Mela rotates among four cities.
4. India had about 172 million Muslims in 2011, and Sri Lanka's 2024 census found large Hindu, Muslim and Christian communities.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Every South Asian country is home to several faiths, and even minority shares can mean millions of people. Option B is true but doesn't address the claim that religion and country line up one-to-one.

    **Concept Tested:** Religious Pluralism
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 9

Adam's Peak in Sri Lanka is visited by Buddhists, Hindus, Muslims and Christians, who each explain its footprint differently. What does this example best show?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. That the four faiths have merged into one religion in Sri Lanka
2. That one faith's explanation of the footprint is the correct one
3. That a single sacred place can hold different meanings for different faiths, an example of religious pluralism
4. That pilgrimage is only important in Buddhism
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Shared sites like Adam's Peak show people of different faiths valuing the same place in their own ways. The faiths remain distinct (so A is wrong), and the book does not rank one explanation above another (so B is wrong).

    **Concept Tested:** Pilgrimage
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

#### Question 10

Pew Research Center's 2021 report on religion in India was titled "Tolerance and Segregation." Which conclusion does the evidence in the chapter best support?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Most Indians value respect for all religions, yet many keep their friendships and marriages within their own faith.
2. Indians of different faiths share no beliefs at all.
3. Religious tension in India has completely disappeared.
4. Only Hindus in India believe in karma.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The survey found that respect for all religions is widely valued, but many people's friends come mostly from their own group, and many oppose interfaith marriage. Option D is contradicted by the finding that 77% of both Hindus and Muslims said they believe in karma.

    **Concept Tested:** Religious Pluralism
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze
