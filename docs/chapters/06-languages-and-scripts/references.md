# References: Languages and Scripts

1. [Eighth Schedule: Languages](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/schedules/languages) - Constitution of India (constitutionofindia.net) - The full list of the 22 languages in the Eighth Schedule, with the original list of 14 from 1949, so you can see which languages were added later.

2. [Article 343: Official language of the Union](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/articles/article-343-official-language-of-the-union/) - Constitution of India (constitutionofindia.net) - The exact constitutional text making Hindi in Devanagari the official language of India's central government, plus the debates behind it. Useful for checking the "national language" myth yourself.

3. [Language: India, States and Union Territories (Paper 1 of 2018), Census of India 2011](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/42458) - Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, India - The official source for India's mother-tongue numbers: speakers of each scheduled language, the 121 languages counted, and the share of people in each language family.

4. [Unicode Character Code Charts](https://www.unicode.org/charts/) - The Unicode Consortium - The official charts for every script in this chapter, from Devanagari and Bengali to Brahmi, Kharoshthi and Thaana. Every character in the chapter and the MicroSim can be checked here.

5. [International Mother Language Day](https://www.unesco.org/en/days/mother-language) - UNESCO - Explains why 21 February is observed worldwide, notes that the idea came from Bangladesh, and describes UNESCO's work on mother-tongue education and endangered languages.

6. [Status of Classical Language: An Explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/oct/doc2024104408501.pdf) - Press Information Bureau, Government of India (October 2024) - Lists India's 11 classical languages and when each was recognized, starting with Tamil on 12 October 2004 and ending with Bangla and four others in October 2024.

7. [Decoding diversity](https://nepalitimes.com/decoding-diversity) - Nepali Times (June 2023) - A readable summary of the language results of Nepal's 2021 census: 124 mother tongues, with Nepali the mother tongue of 44.86% of people.

8. [Punjabi tops as 'most spoken language' in Pakistan](https://www.geo.tv/latest/574766-punjabi-tops-as-most-spoken-language-in-pakistan) - Geo News (November 2024) - Reports Pakistan Bureau of Statistics figures from the 2023 census, including Punjabi as the largest mother tongue and Urdu's growth to 9.25%.

9. [Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script) - Wikipedia - A detailed overview of Brahmi's earliest inscriptions, James Prinsep's decipherment, the debate over its origins, and its many descendants, with links to the scholarly sources.

10. [Hindustani language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language) - Wikipedia - Explains how Hindi and Urdu share one spoken base but differ in script and formal vocabulary, with sources for further reading on the Hindi-Urdu debate.
