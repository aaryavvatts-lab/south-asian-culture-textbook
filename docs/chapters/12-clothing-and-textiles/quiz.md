#### Question 1

Where were the oldest known cotton fibers in the world found?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. In a tomb near Varanasi, India
2. Inside copper beads at Mehrgarh, Pakistan
3. In a weaver's workshop near Dhaka, Bangladesh
4. In a monastery in Bhutan
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Cotton fibers preserved inside copper beads in a burial at Mehrgarh, in Balochistan, Pakistan, date to the first half of the sixth millennium BCE, roughly 7,500 to 8,000 years ago. Dhaka became famous for fine cotton muslin much later, so it is a tempting but wrong choice.

    **Concept Tested:** Cotton in South Asia
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 2

Which of these best defines khadi?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Any cloth made of cotton
2. A silk saree woven in Varanasi
3. Cloth printed with wooden blocks in Sindh
4. Cloth that is both hand-spun and hand-woven
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Khadi is hand-spun and hand-woven cloth, and it can be cotton, silk or wool. Option A is a common misconception: khadi is defined by how it is made, not by the fiber.

    **Concept Tested:** Khadi
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 3

What are the gho and kira?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The national dress of Bhutan for men and women
2. Two styles of saree draping from Nepal
3. Turbans worn by Pashtun men in Afghanistan
4. Silk weaves from Assam
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The gho is the knee-length belted robe worn by men in Bhutan, and the kira is the ankle-length wrapped dress worn by women. Nepal's national dress for men is the daura-suruwal, not the gho.

    **Concept Tested:** Gho and Kira
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 4

Why do ikat patterns usually have soft, feathery edges?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The pattern is painted on with a brush after weaving.
2. The cloth is washed many times to fade the colors.
3. The threads are tie-dyed before weaving and never line up perfectly on the loom.
4. Weavers add extra weft threads one motif at a time.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. In ikat, bundles of thread are tied and dyed in a pattern before they are woven, and small shifts on the loom blur the edges. Option D describes Jamdani, where the pattern is woven in with extra weft threads.

    **Concept Tested:** Ikat
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 5

Why did Gandhi ask Indians to spin and wear khadi?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because khadi was cheaper than any other cloth
2. To make India less dependent on British machine-made cloth and show self-reliance
3. Because British law banned Indians from wearing silk
4. To start a new fashion trend for weddings
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Under colonial rule, Indian cotton was often shipped to British mills and sold back as cloth. Gandhi's swadeshi movement urged people to boycott foreign cloth and spin their own. Option A is wrong: hand-spun yarn was often more expensive than mill yarn.

    **Concept Tested:** Khadi
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 6

What makes the saree and the dhoti "draped" garments rather than "stitched" garments?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. They are long unstitched lengths of cloth wrapped and tucked around the body.
2. They are always made of silk.
3. They are only worn at weddings.
4. They are sewn into a tube before being worn.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Both are rectangles of cloth that are wrapped, pleated and tucked rather than cut and sewn. Option D describes the Bangladeshi lungi, which is often sewn into a tube, and even that is wrapped rather than tailored.

    **Concept Tested:** Saree
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 7

At a community event, you see a man wearing a turban. Based on the chapter, what is the best conclusion?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. He must be Sikh.
2. He must be Muslim.
3. He must be from Rajasthan.
4. You can't tell his religion or region from the turban alone; it's best to ask respectfully if you want to know.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Turbans are worn by Sikhs, many Pashtun men, elders in Rajasthan, some Hindu monks and some Muslim scholars, among others. Option A is the most common mistake: the Sikh dastar has a specific meaning, but not every turban is a dastar.

    **Concept Tested:** Turban (Dastar)
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 8

Your friend's mother, who is from Bangladesh, wears a white conch-shell bangle and a red bangle every day. What do they most likely signify?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. She has completed a pilgrimage.
2. She is a member of a weavers' guild.
3. She is a married Bengali Hindu woman wearing shankha and pola.
4. She is a Sikh wearing a kara.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Many married Bengali Hindu women in Bangladesh and West Bengal wear the white shankha and the red pola, traditionally given at the wedding. Option D is wrong because the Sikh kara is a single plain steel or iron bangle.

    **Concept Tested:** Bangles
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 9

The shalwar kameez is Pakistan's national dress, yet Simran's family from Ludhiana, India, wears it too. What does this best show?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Many South Asian garments are older than today's borders and are shared across countries.
2. Simran's family must have moved from Pakistan recently.
3. The shalwar kameez was invented in India and later copied by Pakistan.
4. National dress laws in Pakistan also apply in India.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The shalwar kameez spread across the Punjab region over centuries, long before the 1947 border, so it is worn on both sides. Option C wrongly turns a shared tradition into a contest over who invented it.

    **Concept Tested:** Shalwar Kameez
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

#### Question 10

Jamdani, ajrak and Banarasi brocade are all patterned cloth. Which comparison is correct?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. All three are printed with wooden blocks.
2. Jamdani and Banarasi patterns are woven in on the loom, while ajrak is block-printed.
3. Jamdani is silk, ajrak is wool and Banarasi is cotton.
4. All three are tie-dyed before weaving.
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Jamdani motifs are woven in with extra weft threads, Banarasi brocade weaves in zari, and ajrak is printed with carved blocks and natural dyes. Option D describes ikat, and none of these three is ikat.

    **Concept Tested:** Jamdani
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze
