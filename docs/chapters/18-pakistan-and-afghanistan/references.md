# References: Pakistan and Afghanistan

1. [Population, total (SP.POP.TOTL)](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL) - World Bank Open Data - The source for the 2025 populations of Pakistan and Afghanistan used in this chapter. You can compare how fast each country's population has grown since 1960.

2. [Khyber Pass](https://www.britannica.com/place/Khyber-Pass) - Encyclopaedia Britannica - A detailed account of the pass's geography (about 53 km from Jamrud to Torkham) and its long history, from Darius I and Ashoka's Buddhists to Babur, the British railway and today's trade.

3. [Durand Line](https://www.britannica.com/event/Durand-Line) - Encyclopaedia Britannica - Explains how the 1893 border between British India and Afghanistan was drawn, why it split the Pashtun homeland, and why Afghan governments have called it void. Useful for the Whose Story? box.

4. [Cultural Landscape and Archaeological Remains of the Bamiyan Valley](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/208/) - UNESCO World Heritage Centre - The official World Heritage listing for Bamiyan, with the heights of the two destroyed Buddhas, the cave monasteries and the valley's place on the Silk Road.

5. [Fort and Shalamar Gardens in Lahore](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/171/) - UNESCO World Heritage Centre - Describes the two Mughal royal complexes in Lahore listed in 1981, with the history of the fort under Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb.

6. [War rugs: Afghanistan's knotted history (large print guide)](https://www.britishmuseum.org/exhibitions/war-rugs-afghanistans-knotted-history/war-rugs-large-print-guide) - British Museum - The full text of a museum exhibition on Afghan carpets, explaining Turkmen and Baluch weaving traditions and how weavers recorded decades of war in their rugs.

7. [The Constitution of Pakistan, Part XII, Chapter 4 (Article 251)](https://www.pakistani.org/pakistan/constitution/part12.ch4.html) - pakistani.org - The text of the article that makes Urdu the national language, lets English be used for official purposes, and allows provinces to promote their own languages.

8. [What is the most spoken language of Pakistan?](https://jang.com.pk/en/25434-what-is-the-most-spoken-language-of-pakistan-news) - Jang (18 November 2024) - A news summary of the 2023 census language results from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, including Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi, Urdu and Balochi shares nationally and by province.

9. [UNESCO says 2.4 million Afghan girls denied access to further education](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/11/unesco-says-2-4-million-afghan-girls-denied-access-to-further-education) - Al Jazeera (11 August 2026) - Reports UNESCO's latest figures on the ban on girls' secondary schooling and women's university study in Afghanistan, with the dates the bans began.

10. [Pashtunwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtunwali) - Wikipedia - An overview of the Pashtun code of conduct and its main principles, such as hospitality, sanctuary, honor and the jirga. Use its citations to find scholarly sources.
