#### Question 1

Which description best fits the word "diaspora"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Any group of tourists visiting a foreign country
2. A population living outside its ancestral homeland that keeps some connection to it
3. People who move from a village to a city inside the same country
4. A government program that sends workers abroad on contracts
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A diaspora lives outside its ancestral homeland but keeps ties to it through family, faith, language, food or identity. Moving from a village to a city inside one country is migration, but it does not create a diaspora, because people have not left the homeland.

    **Concept Tested:** Diaspora
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 2

Why did plantation owners in British sugar colonies begin recruiting indentured workers from India in the 1830s?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. India's government asked Britain to send its farmers abroad
2. Sugar prices had collapsed and planters wanted cheaper machines
3. The workers were fleeing the Partition of India
4. Britain had abolished slavery, and planters wanted a new source of cheap labor
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Britain abolished slavery across its empire by an 1833 act, so planters in places like British Guiana, Trinidad and Mauritius turned to contract workers from British India. Partition happened in 1947, more than a century later, so it cannot explain this wave.

    **Concept Tested:** Indentured Labor
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 3

In 1972, Idi Amin gave South Asians in Uganda how long to leave the country?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 90 days
2. 30 days
3. One year
4. Five years
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. On 4 August 1972, Amin announced that South Asians had 90 days to leave. About 80,000 people were affected, and around 28,000 went to the United Kingdom.

    **Concept Tested:** Uganda Expulsion of 1972
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 4

What did the US Supreme Court decide in *United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind* (1923)?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. South Asians could become citizens if they had served in the army
2. California's ban on Punjabi farmers was unconstitutional
3. South Asians were not "white persons" under the law, so they could not naturalize
4. Sikhs had the right to wear turbans in the military
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The Court ruled unanimously that "white" meant its everyday meaning, so Thind, a South Asian, could not become a naturalized citizen. Thind did later become a citizen in 1936 through a law for World War I veterans, but that was not what the 1923 decision said.

    **Concept Tested:** Bhagat Singh Thind Case
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 5

Why did the Immigration Act of 1965 lead to a large increase in South Asian immigration to the United States?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It ended the national origins quotas that had favored northern and western Europeans
2. It gave every South Asian an automatic green card
3. It created the Asiatic Barred Zone
4. It only allowed immigrants who already spoke English
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The 1965 law replaced national origins quotas with preferences for family reunification and needed skills, which opened the door to South Asian doctors, engineers, students and then their families. The Asiatic Barred Zone came from the 1917 law and did the opposite.

    **Concept Tested:** Immigration Act of 1965
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 6

A worker from Kerala takes a construction job in the UAE. Under the *kafala* system, his visa and legal residence are tied to his employer, and he cannot become a citizen. Which concept from the chapter best describes him?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Indentured Labor
2. Gulf Migrant Workers
3. Punjabi Pioneers in California
4. Indo-Caribbean Communities
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Gulf migrant workers usually go on temporary contracts, often under kafala, without a path to citizenship. Indenture is a tempting choice because both involve contracts far from home, but the indenture system ended around 1920.

    **Concept Tested:** Gulf Migrant Workers
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 7

According to Pew Research Center (2023 data), Bangladeshi American households had a median income of about $78,400, while Indian American households had about $151,200. What does this comparison most directly show?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Bangladeshi Americans work fewer hours than Indian Americans
2. All Asian Americans earn more than other Americans
3. The 1965 law banned Bangladeshi immigrants
4. Treating all South Asian Americans as wealthy hides real differences between groups
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The big gap between groups is one reason the model minority myth is called a myth: it hides real differences and struggles. The data say nothing about hours worked, so option A is an unsupported assumption.

    **Concept Tested:** Model Minority Myth
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 8

In 2025, remittances to Nepal equaled about 32% of the country's GDP (World Bank). Which statement is the best conclusion from that number?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Nepal receives more remittance money than India
2. Most Nepalis live outside Nepal
3. Money sent home by migrants is a very large part of Nepal's economy
4. Nepal's government sends money to workers abroad
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A share of nearly a third of GDP means remittances matter a great deal to Nepali families and the economy. India receives far more money in total (about US$150.7 billion in 2025), so option A confuses share with total amount.

    **Concept Tested:** Remittances
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

#### Question 9

Simran's family friend says, "My uncle came to California from Punjab in 1910, worked on farms, and married a Mexican woman because almost no Punjabi women could come." Which part of the chapter does his story fit?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Punjabi Pioneers in California
2. Post-9/11 Backlash
3. The Immigration Act of 1965
4. British South Asians
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Punjabi men came to the US West Coast in the early 1900s, and many married Mexican and Mexican American women because South Asian women could rarely immigrate and California law banned marriages across racial labels. The 1965 law came more than fifty years later.

    **Concept Tested:** Punjabi Pioneers in California
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 10

Balbir Singh Sodhi, a Sikh man, was killed on 15 September 2001 by an attacker who thought he was an Arab Muslim. What does this case best reveal about the post-9/11 backlash?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Only Muslims were targeted after the attacks
2. Hatred lumped together people of different faiths and backgrounds based on how they looked
3. Hate crimes fell sharply after September 2001
4. Sikh Americans were rarely affected because they are not Muslim
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Sodhi was targeted because of his turban and beard, even though he was not Muslim or Arab, which shows how attackers profiled people by appearance. Option A is wrong because Sikhs and other South Asians were also attacked, and FBI data show anti-Islamic incidents rose, not fell, from 28 in 2000 to 481 in 2001.

    **Concept Tested:** Post-9/11 Backlash
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze
