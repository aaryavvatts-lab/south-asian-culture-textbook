#### Question 1

What is oral history?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A collection of official government documents about a community
2. The practice of recording people's memories of their own lives, in their own words, and keeping them for others to learn from
3. Stories that are made up and passed around without anyone writing them down
4. A news interview with a famous person about current events
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Oral history records people's own memories in their own words and preserves them, with permission, for the future. It is not made-up storytelling (C). It adds personal experience to what official documents (A) leave out.

    **Concept Tested:** Oral History
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 2

Which description matches the term "third culture kids" as Ruth Useem first used it?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Any child of immigrants who settles permanently in a new country
2. Children who speak three languages at home
3. Children of parents from three different countries
4. Children who spent much of their childhood in a culture different from their parents', usually because a parent's job moved the family abroad
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Ruth Useem used the term in the 1960s for children of Americans working abroad. Strictly, children of immigrants who settle in one country (A) aren't usually called third culture kids, although many second-generation teens relate to the idea.

    **Concept Tested:** Third Culture Kids
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 3

In 2020, Hindustan Unilever renamed its skin-lightening cream Fair & Lovely as Glow & Lovely. Which concept from the chapter does this example mainly illustrate?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Colorism
2. Cultural preservation
3. Hyphenated identity
4. Community impact
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The cream was criticized for linking lighter, "fair" skin to beauty and success, which is colorism: favoring lighter skin over darker skin. The renaming was a response to that criticism; it is not an example of preserving a tradition (B).

    **Concept Tested:** Colorism
    **Bloom's Level:** Remember

#### Question 4

Why did many people object to the hyphen in terms like "Indian-American"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Hyphens are grammatically wrong in all English writing
2. The hyphen was required only for South Asian Americans
3. "Hyphenated American" had been used as a put-down suggesting immigrants weren't fully loyal, and some felt the hyphen split them in half
4. The Constitution of India bans hyphens in names
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. From about 1890 to 1920, "hyphenated American" was used as an insult, and many people felt the hyphen made them sound only partly American. In March 2019 the Associated Press dropped the hyphen from dual-heritage terms. Hyphens aren't wrong in general (A); this is about history and identity.

    **Concept Tested:** Hyphenated Identity
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 5

Which statement best explains the difference between cultural appreciation and cultural appropriation?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Appreciation means never taking part in another culture; appropriation means taking part at all
2. Appreciation involves understanding meaning, being invited, giving credit and supporting the community; appropriation takes elements while ignoring meaning, credit or the community
3. Appreciation applies only to food, and appropriation applies only to clothing
4. There is no real difference; both words mean borrowing from another culture
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The chapter separates the two by meaning, invitation, credit and power. Appreciation does not mean staying away entirely (A); wearing a saree to a friend's wedding when invited is an example of appreciation.

    **Concept Tested:** Cultural Appreciation
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 6

Why does the chapter say that preservation is "not the same as freezing"?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Living traditions survive by being practiced and adapted, so a tradition can change and still be itself
2. UNESCO forbids any change to traditions on its lists
3. Traditions only count if they are exactly as they were a thousand years ago
4. Preservation means storing objects in a museum and no longer using them
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Durga Puja in Kolkata looks different from a century ago and is still Durga Puja, and families adapt festivals to new homes. Option C is the "culture is fixed" myth the chapter corrects.

    **Concept Tested:** Cultural Preservation
    **Bloom's Level:** Understand

#### Question 7

Dilan is about to interview his grandfather about growing up in Kandy. Which step should come first?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Start recording audio so nothing is missed
2. Ask the hardest question first while his grandfather has the most energy
3. Post a photo of his grandfather to the club's page to build excitement
4. Explain the project, how the notes will be used and who might see them, and ask whether it's OK, making clear he can skip questions or stop
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Informed consent comes before anything else. Recording without asking (A) skips consent, and posting a photo (C) breaks the privacy rule of sharing nothing without clear permission.

    **Concept Tested:** Informed Consent
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 8

During an interview, Ayesha's grandmother mentions a train ride to Lahore and her voice changes. Ayesha's next planned question is about cricket. What is the best active listening move?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Move straight to the cricket question to stay on schedule
2. Correct her if the date she gives doesn't match the textbook
3. Pause, then gently ask something like "Can you tell me more about that?" while being ready to stop or move on if the memory is painful
4. Tell her own story about a train ride to keep the conversation going
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Active listening means following the speaker's lead, leaving room for silence and reading the room, especially with possibly painful memories. Sticking rigidly to the list (A) misses the story, and correcting memory (B) treats the interview like a test.

    **Concept Tested:** Active Listening
    **Bloom's Level:** Apply

#### Question 9

The Liberation War Museum in Dhaka asks school students to interview elders about 1971 and send in their accounts. How is this most similar to the book's Ask Your Elders method?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Both rely on official government records instead of personal memories
2. Both have teens ask older relatives or community members about their own experiences and write the stories down
3. Both are run only by professional historians
4. Both collect stories only from people who left South Asia
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. In both, young people learn some history, then gather memories from elders in their own words. Option A describes the opposite of oral history. Option D is wrong because the museum's accounts come from people in Bangladesh, not only from people who left.

    **Concept Tested:** Intergenerational Dialogue
    **Bloom's Level:** Analyze

#### Question 10

A club reports two results at the end of the year: "We held 10 meetings" and "Five new members said they finally feel they belong at school." Which analysis is correct?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Both are community impact, because both are numbers
2. Neither matters, because clubs can't measure anything
3. The first is activity; the second is community impact, because it describes a real change in people's lives
4. The first is community impact, because more meetings always means more change
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The chapter defines community impact as the real difference a project makes, not just how busy a group was. Counting meetings (D) measures activity; impact shows up in what changed for people.

    **Concept Tested:** Community Impact
