## A

#### Abugida

A writing system in which each consonant letter carries a built-in vowel, usually a short "a," and other vowels are shown by marks attached to the consonant. A mark called a virama removes the built-in vowel.

**Example:** In Devanagari, the letter क is read "ka" unless a vowel mark or a virama changes it.

**See also:** [Devanagari](#devanagari), [Gurmukhi](#gurmukhi), [Brahmi Script](#brahmi-script)

*Taught in [Chapter 6: Languages and Scripts](chapters/06-languages-and-scripts/index.md).*

#### Active Listening

Giving a speaker full attention and showing understanding by focusing on their words, asking follow-up questions, reflecting back what was heard and leaving room for silence.

**Example:** Saying "So you moved when you were twelve?" before asking the next question is active listening.

**See also:** [Oral History](#oral-history), [Intergenerational Dialogue](#intergenerational-dialogue)

*Taught in [Chapter 22: Bring It Home](chapters/22-bring-it-home/index.md).*

#### Adivasi Communities

India's Indigenous or tribal peoples, many with their own languages, faiths, governance and ways of life tied to forests, hills and land. The government's official term is Scheduled Tribes.

**Script and sound:** आदिवासी, *AH-dee-VAH-see*, "original inhabitants"

**Example:** Many Santali speakers in Jharkhand belong to Adivasi communities.

**See also:** [Austroasiatic Languages](#austroasiatic-languages), [Unity in Diversity](#unity-in-diversity)

*Taught in [Chapter 17: India](chapters/17-india/index.md).*

#### Afghan Carpets

Hand-knotted wool rugs woven in Afghanistan, especially in the north and west, in traditions passed down within families for generations.

**Example:** Afghan carpets are one of the country's best-known crafts and an important source of household income.

**See also:** [Afghanistan](#afghanistan)

*Taught in [Chapter 18: Pakistan and Afghanistan](chapters/18-pakistan-and-afghanistan/index.md).*

#### Afghanistan

A landlocked, mountainous country where South Asia meets Central Asia and Iran, and the eighth member of SAARC, which it joined in 2007. It had about 44 million people in 2025 (World Bank).

**Example:** The nearest sea to Afghanistan, the Arabian Sea, is about 480 km to the south.

**See also:** [Kabul](#kabul), [Dari and Pashto](#dari-and-pashto), [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC)](#south-asian-association-for-regional-cooperation-saarc)

*Taught in [Chapter 18: Pakistan and Afghanistan](chapters/18-pakistan-and-afghanistan/index.md).*

#### Ahimsa

Non-violence: not harming any living being, in action, word or thought. Hindus and Buddhists value it, and Jains place it at the very center of their lives.

**Script and sound:** अहिंसा, *uh-HIM-sah*

**Example:** Many Jains avoid root vegetables such as potatoes, onions and garlic to avoid harming tiny living things.

**See also:** [Jainism](#jainism), [Satyagraha](#satyagraha), [Vegetarianism in South Asia](#vegetarianism-in-south-asia)

*Taught in [Chapter 5: Faiths of South Asia](chapters/05-faiths-of-south-asia/index.md).*

#### Akbar

The third Mughal emperor, who ruled from 1556 to 1605 and built the Mughal state into a lasting empire stretching from Kabul to Bengal.

**Example:** Akbar became emperor as a teenager.

**See also:** [Mughal Empire](#mughal-empire), [Akbar and Birbal Tales](#akbar-and-birbal-tales)

*Taught in [Chapter 3: Empires and Encounters](chapters/03-empires-and-encounters/index.md).*

#### Akbar and Birbal Tales

Funny stories about the Mughal emperor Akbar and his quick-witted minister Birbal, who solves impossible puzzles and outsmarts jealous courtiers. Birbal was real, but most of the tales are later folk creations.

**Example:** In many tales, Birbal answers a trick question from Akbar with a clever twist.

**See also:** [Akbar](#akbar), [Oral Tradition](#oral-tradition)

*Taught in [Chapter 14: Stories and Literature](chapters/14-stories-and-literature/index.md).*

#### Al-Biruni

A Muslim scholar born in Khwarazm in Central Asia (973 to 1048) who wrote one of the most careful early books about South Asian science and society after going to Ghazni, in today's Afghanistan.

**Example:** Al-Biruni measured the positions of towns in the Punjab during Mahmud of Ghazni's expeditions.

**See also:** [Hindu-Arabic Numerals](#hindu-arabic-numerals)

*Taught in [Chapter 16: Science, Math, and Ideas](chapters/16-science-math-ideas/index.md).*

#### All-India Muslim League

A political party founded in 1906 in Dhaka to represent the political interests of Muslims in British India. In 1940 it adopted the Lahore Resolution, later called the Pakistan Resolution.

**Example:** Muhammad Ali Jinnah led the All-India Muslim League in the years before Partition.

**See also:** [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](#muhammad-ali-jinnah), [Partition of 1947](#partition-of-1947), [Indian National Congress](#indian-national-congress)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### Allama Iqbal

Sir Muhammad Iqbal (1877 to 1938), a poet and philosopher born in Sialkot who wrote in Urdu and Persian and whose 1930 Allahabad Address called for a separate political home for Muslims in northwest British India. *Allama* means "learned scholar."

**Example:** Pakistan was founded nine years after Iqbal's death.

**See also:** [All-India Muslim League](#all-india-muslim-league), [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](#muhammad-ali-jinnah)

*Taught in [Chapter 14: Stories and Literature](chapters/14-stories-and-literature/index.md).*

#### Allyship

The ongoing practice of supporting a group one is not part of by listening, learning, showing up and using one's voice or access to back that group, while letting its members lead.

**Example:** Ben joining the South Asian Student Association and helping set up events is allyship.

**See also:** [Cultural Appreciation](#cultural-appreciation), [Community Organizing](#community-organizing)

*Taught in [Chapter 22: Bring It Home](chapters/22-bring-it-home/index.md).*

#### Anand Karaj

The Sikh wedding ceremony, "blissful ceremony," held in front of the Guru Granth Sahib, at the center of which the couple walks around the scripture four times as the four hymns of the *laavan* are sung.

**Script and sound:** ਅਨੰਦ ਕਾਰਜ, *uh-NUND KAA-raj*

**Example:** Many Sikh weddings take place in the morning at a gurdwara.

**See also:** [Guru Granth Sahib](#guru-granth-sahib), [Weddings](#weddings), [Nikah](#nikah)

*Taught in [Chapter 13: Family, Names, and Life Events](chapters/13-family-names-life-events/index.md).*

#### Anglo-Afghan Wars

Three wars that Britain fought in Afghanistan, in 1839 to 1842, 1878 to 1880, and 1919, driven by British fear that the Russian Empire would use Afghanistan as a route to India.

**Example:** After the third war, the 1919 Treaty of Rawalpindi recognized Afghanistan's full independence.

**See also:** [Afghanistan](#afghanistan), [British Raj](#british-raj)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### Anuradhapura

An ancient royal capital in the dry north-central plains of Sri Lanka and a holy city for Buddhists, which flourished for about 1,300 years until it was abandoned after an invasion in 993 CE.

**Script and sound:** *uh-noo-RAH-duh-POO-ruh*

**Example:** Pilgrims still visit Anuradhapura's ancient stupas.

**See also:** [Sri Lankan Kingdoms](#sri-lankan-kingdoms), [Stupa](#stupa)

*Taught in [Chapter 19: Bangladesh and Sri Lanka](chapters/19-bangladesh-and-sri-lanka/index.md).*

#### Arranged Marriage

A marriage in which parents or other relatives help find and suggest a partner, and the couple then decides whether to go ahead. It is common in South Asia alongside "love marriages," where the couple meets on their own.

**Example:** Parents might introduce their son to a family friend's daughter, and the two meet several times before deciding.

**See also:** [Weddings](#weddings), [Gender Roles and Change](#gender-roles-and-change)

*Taught in [Chapter 13: Family, Names, and Life Events](chapters/13-family-names-life-events/index.md).*

#### Aryabhata

A mathematician and astronomer (476 to 550 CE) who worked at Kusumapura during the time of the Gupta kings and finished his verse book, the *Aryabhatiya*, in 499 at age 23.

**Example:** Aryabhata wrote the *Aryabhatiya* in verse so students could memorize it.

**See also:** [Brahmagupta](#brahmagupta), [Gupta Empire](#gupta-empire)

*Taught in [Chapter 16: Science, Math, and Ideas](chapters/16-science-math-ideas/index.md).*

#### Ashoka

The third Maurya emperor, grandson of Chandragupta, who ruled from about 268 to 232 BCE over an empire stretching from present-day Afghanistan to present-day Bangladesh.

**Example:** Ashoka had his messages carved on rocks and stone pillars across his empire.

**See also:** [Maurya Empire](#maurya-empire), [Edicts of Ashoka](#edicts-of-ashoka)

*Taught in [Chapter 2: Ancient Roots](chapters/02-ancient-roots/index.md).*

#### Ashura

The 10th day of Muharram, the first month of the Islamic year. For Shia Muslims it is a solemn day of mourning for Husayn ibn Ali, killed at Karbala in 680 CE, and many Sunni Muslims fast on it.

**Script and sound:** عاشورا, *aa-SHOO-rah*, "the tenth" (Persian)

**Example:** In Dhaka, the Hussaini Dalan is the center of Muharram gatherings.

**See also:** [Sunni and Shia Islam](#sunni-and-shia-islam), [Islamic Lunar Calendar](#islamic-lunar-calendar)

*Taught in [Chapter 7: Festivals Through the Year](chapters/07-festivals/index.md).*

#### Atolls

Rings of coral reef and small low islands surrounding a shallow lagoon. The English word comes from the Dhivehi *atholhu*.

**Script and sound:** އަތޮޅު, *atholhu* (*A-tho-lu*)

**Example:** The islands of the Maldives are grouped into 26 atolls.

**See also:** [Maldives](#maldives), [Dhivehi](#dhivehi)

*Taught in [Chapter 20: Nepal, Bhutan, and the Maldives](chapters/20-nepal-bhutan-maldives/index.md).*

#### Attan

A Pashtun circle dance widely called the national dance of Afghanistan, in which dancers move around a drummer, clapping and spinning as the beat speeds up.

**Script and sound:** اتڼ, *uh-TUN*

**Example:** Attan is often danced at Afghan weddings, including in communities abroad.

**See also:** [Folk Dance](#folk-dance), [Afghanistan](#afghanistan)

*Taught in [Chapter 10: Dance and Theater](chapters/10-dance-and-theater/index.md).*

#### Austroasiatic Languages

A language family spread from eastern India to Vietnam and Cambodia. In South Asia its best-known members are the Munda languages, spoken mainly by Adivasi communities, and Khasi in Meghalaya.

**Example:** Santali, written in its own Ol Chiki script, was added to India's Eighth Schedule in 2003.

**See also:** [Adivasi Communities](#adivasi-communities), [Language Family](#language-family)

*Taught in [Chapter 6: Languages and Scripts](chapters/06-languages-and-scripts/index.md).*

#### Ayurveda

A traditional medical system from South Asia, based on old Sanskrit texts, that uses diet, herbs, daily routines, exercise and sometimes minerals to try to keep the body in balance. This is a description of a tradition, not medical advice.

**Script and sound:** आयुर्वेद, *AA-yur-vay-duh*, "knowledge of life"

**Example:** India has a government ministry, AYUSH, for traditional systems including Ayurveda.

**See also:** [Unani Medicine](#unani-medicine), [Yoga](#yoga)

*Taught in [Chapter 16: Science, Math, and Ideas](chapters/16-science-math-ideas/index.md).*

## B

#### B. R. Ambedkar

Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (1891 to 1956), a lawyer, economist and political leader born into a Dalit family, who fought for the rights of Dalits and chaired the committee that drafted India's Constitution.

**Example:** Ambedkar earned doctorates from Columbia University and the London School of Economics.

**See also:** [Constitution of India](#constitution-of-india), [Caste Discrimination Laws](#caste-discrimination-laws)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### Balochistan

Pakistan's westernmost and largest province by area, bordering Iran and Afghanistan, with Quetta as its capital. It is the most thinly populated province.

**Example:** Mehrgarh, one of South Asia's earliest farming villages, is in Balochistan.

**See also:** [Provinces of Pakistan](#provinces-of-pakistan), [Mehrgarh](#mehrgarh)

*Taught in [Chapter 18: Pakistan and Afghanistan](chapters/18-pakistan-and-afghanistan/index.md).*

#### Bangladesh Liberation War

The 1971 war in which East Pakistan fought for and won independence from Pakistan, becoming Bangladesh. Independence was declared on 26 March 1971 and the war ended on 16 December 1971.

**Script and sound:** মুক্তিযুদ্ধ, *MOOK-tee-jud-dho*, "liberation war"

**Example:** About 10 million refugees fled to India during the Bangladesh Liberation War.

**See also:** [People's Republic of Bangladesh](#peoples-republic-of-bangladesh), [Language Movement of 1952](#language-movement-of-1952)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### Bangles

Rigid bracelets, usually worn in sets, made from glass, metal, shell, lac, plastic or other materials, with a very long history in the region.

**Script and sound:** चूड़ी, *CHOO-ree* (Hindi)

**Example:** The bronze "Dancing Girl" from Mohenjo-daro wears a stack of bangles on her left arm.

**See also:** [Bindi](#bindi), [Harappa and Mohenjo-daro](#harappa-and-mohenjo-daro)

*Taught in [Chapter 12: Clothing and Textiles](chapters/12-clothing-and-textiles/index.md).*

#### Battle of Plassey

The battle on 23 June 1757 near Plassey (Palashi) in Bengal in which East India Company forces under Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah, the Nawab of Bengal, largely because the Nawab's commander Mir Jafar kept his troops out of the fight.

**Example:** After the battle, the Company made Mir Jafar the new Nawab of Bengal.

**See also:** [East India Company](#east-india-company)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### Baul Music

The music of the Bauls, wandering singers of Bangladesh and West Bengal who draw on both Sufi Islam and Vaishnava Hindu devotion, and many of whom reject divisions of caste and religion.

**Script and sound:** বাউল, *BAH-ool*

**Example:** Many Baul singers play the ektara, a one-stringed instrument of gourd and bamboo.

**See also:** [Folk Music](#folk-music), [Sufism](#sufism)

*Taught in [Chapter 9: Music](chapters/09-music/index.md).*

#### Bay of Bengal

The northeastern part of the Indian Ocean, lying east of India and south of Bangladesh, with Myanmar on its eastern side and Sri Lanka at its southwestern edge. It covers about 2.2 million square km.

**Example:** The Ganga, Brahmaputra, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri rivers all flow into the Bay of Bengal.

**See also:** [Indian Ocean](#indian-ocean), [Sundarbans](#sundarbans)

*Taught in [Chapter 1: What Is South Asia?](chapters/01-what-is-south-asia/index.md).*

#### Bengal Delta

The vast, low plain built by the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna rivers where they flow into the Bay of Bengal, the largest river delta in the world. It is also called the Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta.

**Example:** Most of Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal sit on the Bengal Delta.

**See also:** [Sundarbans](#sundarbans), [Rivers and Floods](#rivers-and-floods)

*Taught in [Chapter 19: Bangladesh and Sri Lanka](chapters/19-bangladesh-and-sri-lanka/index.md).*

#### Bengal Famine of 1943

A famine during the Second World War in the province of Bengal and parts of Orissa (now Odisha) in which very large numbers of people died of starvation and disease. Estimates of the death toll range from about 800,000 to 3.8 million.

**Example:** The economic historian Cormac Ó Gráda describes the scholarly consensus as about 2.1 million deaths.

**See also:** [British Raj](#british-raj)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### Bengali Language

An Indo-Aryan language, called Bangla by its speakers, that is the official and national language of Bangladesh and an official language of West Bengal, Tripura and Assam's Barak Valley.

**Script and sound:** বাংলা, *BAANG-laa*

**Example:** In India's 2011 census about 97 million people spoke Bangla as their mother tongue, second only to Hindi.

**See also:** [Language Movement of 1952](#language-movement-of-1952), [Ekushey February](#ekushey-february)

*Taught in [Chapter 6: Languages and Scripts](chapters/06-languages-and-scripts/index.md).*

#### Bhagat Singh Thind Case

*United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind* (1923), a US Supreme Court decision that ruled South Asians were not "white persons" and therefore could not become naturalized citizens.

**Example:** At the time, US law allowed only "free white persons" and people of African descent to naturalize.

**See also:** [Punjabi Pioneers in California](#punjabi-pioneers-in-california), [Immigration Act of 1965](#immigration-act-of-1965)

*Taught in [Chapter 21: The South Asian Diaspora](chapters/21-the-diaspora/index.md).*

#### Bhagavad Gita

A poem of about 700 verses in the sixth book of the Mahabharata, "God's song," in which Krishna answers the warrior Arjuna's doubts before battle in a conversation about duty, action, devotion and the self.

**Script and sound:** भगवद्गीता, *BHUG-uh-vud GEE-taa*

**Example:** Many Hindus read the Bhagavad Gita for guidance on how to act rightly.

**See also:** [Mahabharata](#mahabharata), [Dharma](#dharma)

*Taught in [Chapter 14: Stories and Literature](chapters/14-stories-and-literature/index.md).*

#### Bhakti Movement

A wave of devotional poetry and practice within Hinduism that taught that loving devotion to God is open to everyone, whatever their caste, gender or education. It began in the Tamil country around the 6th century CE and spread north over the next thousand years.

**Script and sound:** भक्ति, *BHUK-tee*, "devotion"

**Example:** The Alvars, devoted to Vishnu, and the Nayanars, devoted to Shiva, were early Tamil poet-saints of the Bhakti Movement.

**See also:** [Hinduism](#hinduism), [Kabir](#kabir), [Sufism](#sufism)

*Taught in [Chapter 3: Empires and Encounters](chapters/03-empires-and-encounters/index.md).*

#### Bhangra

A folk dance and music of Punjab, traditionally linked with the harvest and festivals like Vaisakhi, driven by the dhol, a large two-sided drum.

**Script and sound:** ਭੰਗੜਾ, *BHUNG-ra*

**Example:** Bhangra is danced on both sides of the India-Pakistan border in Punjab.

**See also:** [Folk Music](#folk-music), [Vaisakhi](#vaisakhi), [Global South Asian Pop](#global-south-asian-pop)

*Taught in [Chapter 9: Music](chapters/09-music/index.md).*

#### Bharatanatyam

A classical dance from Tamil Nadu, closely linked for centuries with Hindu temples and royal courts of the Tamil region. Its older name was *Sadir*.

**Script and sound:** பரதநாட்டியம், *BHUH-ruh-tuh-NAHT-yum*

**Example:** The Thanjavur Quartet organized much of the order of pieces still used in Bharatanatyam today.

**See also:** [Classical Dance](#classical-dance), [Mudra](#mudra)

*Taught in [Chapter 10: Dance and Theater](chapters/10-dance-and-theater/index.md).*

#### Bhutan

A small, mountainous kingdom of about 800,000 people (2025, World Bank) in the eastern Himalaya, between India and China's Tibet region, with Thimphu as its capital. Bhutanese call it Druk Yul, "Land of the Thunder Dragon."

**Example:** The thunder dragon appears on Bhutan's national flag.

**See also:** [Dzong](#dzong), [Gross National Happiness](#gross-national-happiness), [Dzongkha](#dzongkha)

*Taught in [Chapter 20: Nepal, Bhutan, and the Maldives](chapters/20-nepal-bhutan-maldives/index.md).*

#### Bindi

A dot, traditionally red or maroon, worn in the center of the forehead, often between the eyebrows. The word comes from the Sanskrit *bindu*, "point" or "drop."

**Script and sound:** बिंदी, *BIN-dee*

**Example:** Many people today wear small stick-on bindis in every color and shape.

**See also:** [Bangles](#bangles), [Cultural Appropriation](#cultural-appropriation)

*Taught in [Chapter 12: Clothing and Textiles](chapters/12-clothing-and-textiles/index.md).*

#### Biryani

A dish of rice layered and slow-cooked with meat or vegetables, yogurt and spices, often with saffron. The name comes from the Persian *biryan*, "fried" or "roasted."

**Script and sound:** بریانی, *bir-YAH-nee* (Urdu)

**Example:** The *Ain-i-Akbari*, written at Akbar's court in the 1590s, lists biryani alongside pilau.

**See also:** [Indo-Persian Culture](#indo-persian-culture), [Regional Cuisines](#regional-cuisines)

*Taught in [Chapter 8: Food and the Table](chapters/08-food-and-the-table/index.md).*

#### Block Printing

A way of printing patterns on cloth by carving a design into a wooden block, dipping it in dye and stamping it by hand over and over.

**Example:** A cloth with three colors may need three carved blocks, lined up by eye.

**See also:** [Cotton in South Asia](#cotton-in-south-asia)

*Taught in [Chapter 11: Art, Architecture, and Craft](chapters/11-art-architecture-craft/index.md).*

#### Bollywood

The popular name, blending "Bombay" and "Hollywood," for the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai. It refers to Hindi films only, not all Indian or South Asian films.

**Example:** A Hindi film with big stars and several song-and-dance numbers, made in Mumbai, is a Bollywood film.

**See also:** [Regional Cinemas](#regional-cinemas), [Playback Singing](#playback-singing), [Film Music (Filmi)](#film-music-filmi)

*Taught in [Chapter 15: Cinema, Cricket, and Pop Culture](chapters/15-cinema-cricket-pop-culture/index.md).*

#### Brahmagupta

An astronomer and mathematician (born 598 CE) who wrote the *Brahmasphutasiddhanta* in 628 CE at Bhillamala and later led the observatory at Ujjain.

**Example:** Brahmagupta treated zero as a number with its own rules.

**See also:** [Zero](#zero), [Aryabhata](#aryabhata)

*Taught in [Chapter 16: Science, Math, and Ideas](chapters/16-science-math-ideas/index.md).*

#### Brahmaputra River

A braided river about 3,000 km (1,900 miles) long that begins in Tibet, cuts south through the Himalaya into India's Arunachal Pradesh, flows through the Assam Valley and enters Bangladesh, where it is called the Jamuna.

**Example:** In Tibet, near Mount Kailash, the same river is called the Yarlung Tsangpo.

**See also:** [Bengal Delta](#bengal-delta), [Rivers and Floods](#rivers-and-floods)

*Taught in [Chapter 1: What Is South Asia?](chapters/01-what-is-south-asia/index.md).*

#### Brahmi Numerals

The number signs written with the Brahmi script, which appear on inscriptions from the 3rd century BCE.

**Example:** An edict of Ashoka at Sasaram, from about 250 BCE, writes the number 256 in Brahmi numerals.

**See also:** [Brahmi Script](#brahmi-script), [Hindu-Arabic Numerals](#hindu-arabic-numerals)

*Taught in [Chapter 16: Science, Math, and Ideas](chapters/16-science-math-ideas/index.md).*

#### Brahmi Script

The ancient script that is the ancestor of most scripts used in South Asia today, and of many in Southeast Asia and Tibet. It was in use by the 3rd century BCE.

**Example:** The Edicts of Ashoka are the best-known early examples of Brahmi, deciphered by James Prinsep in the 1830s.

**See also:** [Edicts of Ashoka](#edicts-of-ashoka), [Abugida](#abugida), [Brahmi Numerals](#brahmi-numerals)

*Taught in [Chapter 6: Languages and Scripts](chapters/06-languages-and-scripts/index.md).*

#### British Raj

Direct rule of South Asia by the British Crown from 1858 to 1947, run by a Viceroy appointed in London. *Raj* means "rule" in Hindi-Urdu.

**Example:** The Viceroy governed first from Calcutta (now Kolkata) and later from New Delhi.

**See also:** [Revolt of 1857](#revolt-of-1857), [Princely States](#princely-states), [Partition of 1947](#partition-of-1947)

*Taught in [Chapter 4: Colonialism, Independence, and Partition](chapters/04-colonialism-and-partition/index.md).*

#### British South Asians

People in the United Kingdom with roots in South Asia, a community that grew sharply in the 1950s and 1960s when Britain needed workers to rebuild after World War II.

**Example:** As Commonwealth citizens, people from India and Pakistan could come to Britain fairly easily in that period.

**See also:** [Diaspora](#diaspora), [Uganda Expulsion of 1972](#uganda-expulsion-of-1972)

*Taught in [Chapter 21: The South Asian Diaspora](chapters/21-the-diaspora/index.md).*

#### Buddhas of Bamiyan

Two giant standing statues of the Buddha, 55 m and 38 m tall, carved into a sandstone cliff in Afghanistan's Bamiyan Valley in the late 500s and early 600s CE and destroyed by the Taliban in March 2001.

**Example:** Empty niches in the Bamiyan cliff show where the statues once stood.

**See also:** [Afghanistan](#afghanistan), [Buddhism](#buddhism)

*Taught in [Chapter 18: Pakistan and Afghanistan](chapters/18-pakistan-and-afghanistan/index.md).*

#### Buddhism

The faith based on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama, called the Buddha ("awakened one"), whom Buddhists believe found the path to freedom from suffering.

**Example:** Buddhists believe the Buddha reached enlightenment while meditating under a tree at Bodh Gaya, in present-day India.

**See also:** [Theravada and Vajrayana](#theravada-and-vajrayana), [Lumbini](#lumbini), [Vesak](#vesak)

*Taught in [Chapter 5: Faiths of South Asia](chapters/05-faiths-of-south-asia/index.md).*

## C

#### C. V. Raman

Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman (1888 to 1970), a physicist from Tamil Nadu who discovered that light scattered by molecules returns partly with a slightly different color, now called the Raman effect. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930.

**Example:** Scientists today use Raman's discovery to identify substances.

#### Calendar Conversion

Finding the date in one calendar that matches a date in another, such as working out which Gregorian day begins a month of the Islamic or a lunisolar calendar.

**Example:** Working out which Gregorian day will be the first of Shawwal, when Eid al-Fitr begins, is calendar conversion.

**See also:** [Lunisolar Calendar](#lunisolar-calendar), [Islamic Lunar Calendar](#islamic-lunar-calendar)

#### Calligraphy

The art of beautiful writing, which holds a special place in Islamic art because many Muslims believe the words of the Qur'an deserve the most beautiful form possible.

**Example:** The Taj Mahal's gateways are framed with Qur'anic verses in a flowing script called *thuluth*.

#### Carnatic Music

The classical music of southern India and of Tamil communities in Sri Lanka and around the world, centered on composed songs, many of them devotional, in languages such as Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Sanskrit and Malayalam.

#### Carrom

A tabletop game of Indian origin in which players flick a heavy disc, the striker, to knock smaller wooden pieces into the four corner pockets of a square board.

Taking an element of another group's culture, usually by someone from a more dominant group, in a way that ignores its meaning, gives no credit, or turns it into a costume or product without the community's involvement.

Split pulses, the dried seeds of plants like lentils, peas and beans, and the soupy or thick dish made from them.

#### Dari and Pashto

Afghanistan's two official national languages, a status first set out in the 1964 constitution. Dari is the form of Persian spoken in Afghanistan; Pashto is the language of the Pashtuns on both sides of the Afghanistan-Pakistan border.

**Script and sound:** دری, *dah-REE* (Dari); پښتو, *PUSH-toh* (Pashto)

**Example:** Afghans of different groups often use Dari to talk to each other.

#### Dashain and Tihar

Nepal's two biggest festivals, about three weeks apart in the autumn. Dashain is a 15-day festival that falls on the same autumn days as Navaratri, and Tihar is a five-day festival of lights.

**Script and sound:** दशैं, *duh-SHAIN* (Dashain); तिहार, *tee-HAAR* (Tihar)

**Example:** On Kukur Tihar, the second day of Tihar, dogs get flower garlands and treats.

#### Deccan Plateau

A large raised tableland covering much of central and southern India, with an average height of about 600 m (2,000 ft). Its name comes from the Sanskrit word for "south."

#### Delhi Sultanate

A series of Muslim-ruled kingdoms based in Delhi that controlled much of northern South Asia from 1206 to 1526 under five dynasties. A *sultan* is a king, and a sultanate is the land he rules.

A Buddhist monument shaped like a dome or mound, built over relics or sacred objects. Buddhists honor it by walking around it clockwise.

**Script and sound:** स्तूप, *STOO-puh*

**Example:** A classic stupa has a solid round dome topped by a railing and a pole with umbrella-like discs.

**See also:** [Buddhism](#buddhism), [Hindu Temple Architecture](#hindu-temple-architecture)

*Taught in [Chapter 11: Art, Architecture, and Craft](chapters/11-art-architecture-craft/index.md).*

#### Sufism

The inward, devotional tradition of Islam, focused on the love of God and drawing closer to God through prayer, remembrance and a teacher's guidance. A Sufi usually belongs to an order, a chain of teachers and students.

**Script and sound:** صوفی, *SOO-fee* (Persian)

**Example:** Qawwali is devotional music that grew out of Sufi gatherings.

**See also:** [Qawwali](#qawwali), [Islam in South Asia](#islam-in-south-asia), [Bhakti Movement](#bhakti-movement)

*Taught in [Chapter 3: Empires and Encounters](chapters/03-empires-and-encounters/index.md).*

#### Sundarbans

The world's largest mangrove forest, on the coast of the Bengal Delta, shared by Bangladesh and India. Its name means "beautiful forest" in Bangla.

**Script and sound:** সুন্দরবন, *SHOON-dor-bon*

**Example:** The name may refer to the sundari trees that grow in the Sundarbans.

**See also:** [Bengal Delta](#bengal-delta), [Bay of Bengal](#bay-of-bengal)

*Taught in [Chapter 19: Bangladesh and Sri Lanka](chapters/19-bangladesh-and-sri-lanka/index.md).*

#### Sunni and Shia Islam

The two main branches of Islam, which separated over a disagreement about who should lead the Muslim community after the Prophet Muhammad died in 632. Sunni Muslims accept Abu Bakr as the first caliph; Shia Muslims hold that the Prophet named Ali as his successor.

**Example:** Ashura is a day of mourning for Shia Muslims and a day of fasting for many Sunni Muslims.

**See also:** [Islam in South Asia](#islam-in-south-asia), [Ashura](#ashura)

*Taught in [Chapter 5: Faiths of South Asia](chapters/05-faiths-of-south-asia/index.md).*

#### Surnames

Family names passed down from one generation to the next. In much of South Asia they exist but do not always work as they do in the United States, and some people have none.

**Example:** The Sri Lankan surname Perera dates from Portuguese rule in the 1500s and 1600s.

**See also:** [Naming Traditions](#naming-traditions)

*Taught in [Chapter 13: Family, Names, and Life Events](chapters/13-family-names-life-events/index.md).*

#### Sushruta Samhita

An old Sanskrit medical text linked to the physician Sushruta, famous for its detailed descriptions of surgical instruments and operations.

**Example:** It describes rebuilding a damaged nose using a flap of skin from the cheek.

**See also:** [Ayurveda](#ayurveda)

*Taught in [Chapter 16: Science, Math, and Ideas](chapters/16-science-math-ideas/index.md).*

## T

#### Tabla

A pair of hand drums that is the main drum of Hindustani music: the smaller wooden dayan produces clear tuned tones, and the larger bayan produces deep bass sounds.

**Script and sound:** तबला, *TUB-la*

**Example:** The black circle of paste on each tabla head gives the drum its ringing tone.

**See also:** [Tala](#tala), [Hindustani Music](#hindustani-music)

*Taught in [Chapter 9: Music](chapters/09-music/index.md).*

#### Tadka

The cooking step called tempering, in which whole spices, and sometimes garlic, dried chili or curry leaves, are fried briefly in hot oil or ghee and poured over a finished dish.

**Script and sound:** तड़का, *TUD-kah*

**Example:** In Bangla the same step is called *phoron*, and in Urdu *baghaar*.

**See also:** [Masala](#masala), [Dal](#dal)

*Taught in [Chapter 8: Food and the Table](chapters/08-food-and-the-table/index.md).*

#### Taj Mahal

A white marble mausoleum in Agra built between about 1631 and 1648 by order of the emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1983.

**Example:** Shah Jahan himself was later buried in the Taj Mahal beside Mumtaz Mahal.

**See also:** [Mughal Architecture](#mughal-architecture), [Mughal Empire](#mughal-empire)

*Taught in [Chapter 3: Empires and Encounters](chapters/03-empires-and-encounters/index.md).*

#### Tala

A repeating cycle of beats that organizes time in a piece of music, counted with claps, waves and finger taps. The first beat of the cycle, the *sam*, is its most important moment.

**Script and sound:** ताल, *TAAL*

**Example:** Musicians may improvise across the beat but aim to land together on sam at the end of each tala cycle.

**See also:** [Raga](#raga), [Tabla](#tabla)

*Taught in [Chapter 9: Music](chapters/09-music/index.md).*

#### Tamil Language

A Dravidian language spoken by about 69 million people in India in 2011, mostly in Tamil Nadu, and one of the two official and national languages of Sri Lanka. Its literature goes back roughly two thousand years.

**Script and sound:** தமிழ், *THUH-mizh*

**Example:** Tamil is also one of the official languages of Singapore.

**See also:** [Dravidian Languages](#dravidian-languages), [Sangam Literature](#sangam-literature), [Sri Lankan Tamils](#sri-lankan-tamils)

*Taught in [Chapter 6: Languages and Scripts](chapters/06-languages-and-scripts/index.md).*

#### Terracotta

Clay that is shaped and then fired, usually to an orange-red color. The Italian word means "baked earth."

**Example:** In the Bengal delta, temple walls were covered with terracotta panels showing gods, epics and daily life.

**See also:** [Hindu Temple Architecture](#hindu-temple-architecture)

*Taught in [Chapter 11: Art, Architecture, and Craft](chapters/11-art-architecture-craft/index.md).*

#### Thali

A large round plate, usually metal, holding a complete meal: a staple in the middle and several small dishes in bowls called katori around it. The word means both the plate and the meal.

**Script and sound:** थाली, *TAH-lee*, "plate"

**Example:** A Gujarati thali might hold rice and roti with dal, vegetables, pickle and a sweet.

**See also:** [Regional Cuisines](#regional-cuisines), [Dal](#dal)

*Taught in [Chapter 8: Food and the Table](chapters/08-food-and-the-table/index.md).*

#### Thangka

A Buddhist painting on cotton or silk that can be rolled up like a scroll, showing a deity, a scene from the Buddha's life or a mandala. It is used for teaching and meditation.

**Script and sound:** ཐང་ཀ, *TAHNG-kah* (Tibetan)

**Example:** Thangkas are made in Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet and the Himalayan parts of India.

**See also:** [Theravada and Vajrayana](#theravada-and-vajrayana)

*Taught in [Chapter 11: Art, Architecture, and Craft](chapters/11-art-architecture-craft/index.md).*

#### Thar Desert

A dry region of sandy plains and low hills, also called the Great Indian Desert, that lies mostly in the Indian state of Rajasthan and partly in Pakistan's Sindh and Punjab provinces. It usually gets less than 500 mm (20 inches) of rain a year.

**Example:** About 85 percent of the Thar Desert lies in India.

**See also:** [Monsoon](#monsoon), [Sindh](#sindh)

*Taught in [Chapter 1: What Is South Asia?](chapters/01-what-is-south-asia/index.md).*

#### The Vedas

Four collections of sacred hymns, chants, ritual instructions and teachings composed in an early form of Sanskrit, which are the oldest scriptures of Hinduism.

**Script and sound:** वेद, *VAY-duh*, "knowledge"

**Example:** The Rig Veda is the oldest of the four Vedas.

**See also:** [Vedic Period](#vedic-period), [Hinduism](#hinduism)

*Taught in [Chapter 2: Ancient Roots](chapters/02-ancient-roots/index.md).*

#### Theravada and Vajrayana

Two of the main schools of Buddhism. Theravada, "teaching of the elders," is the school of Sri Lanka and follows the Pali Canon; Vajrayana, the "diamond" or "thunderbolt" vehicle, became the Buddhism of Tibet, Bhutan and Himalayan Nepal.

**Example:** A monk in Kandy follows Theravada, while a monk in a Bhutanese dzong follows Vajrayana.

**See also:** [Buddhism](#buddhism), [Thangka](#thangka)

*Taught in [Chapter 5: Faiths of South Asia](chapters/05-faiths-of-south-asia/index.md).*

#### Third Culture Kids

People who spent a large part of their childhood living in a culture different from their parents', usually because a parent's job moved the family between countries. The sociologist Ruth Useem first used the term in the 1960s.

**Example:** A child of diplomats who grew up in three countries is a third culture kid.

**See also:** [Hyphenated Identity](#hyphenated-identity), [Cultural Identity](#cultural-identity)

*Taught in [Chapter 22: Bring It Home](chapters/22-bring-it-home/index.md).*

#### Tibeto-Burman Languages

The South Asian members of the Sino-Tibetan family, spoken mostly in the Himalaya and in India's northeast. India's 2011 census counted 66 of them, more than any other family.

**Example:** Dzongkha in Bhutan and Sherpa and Newar in Nepal are Tibeto-Burman languages.

**See also:** [Dzongkha](#dzongkha), [Language Family](#language-family)

*Taught in [Chapter 6: Languages and Scripts](chapters/06-languages-and-scripts/index.md).*

#### Transliteration

Writing a word from one script in another script, letter by letter, so that a reader could rebuild the original spelling, often using extra marks.

**Example:** In one widely used system, दीवाली becomes *dīvālī*.

**See also:** [Phonetic Respelling](#phonetic-respelling), [Devanagari](#devanagari)

#### Transnational Families

Families whose members live in two or more countries but stay closely connected, sharing money, decisions, care and daily life across borders.

**Example:** A grandmother in Dhaka who joins dinner every night by video call is part of a transnational family.

#### Truck Art

The tradition of decorating trucks and buses with paintings, calligraphy, carved wood, mirrors and reflective strips, best known in Pakistan.

**Example:** A decorated Pakistani truck may carry mountain landscapes, poetry and a tall carved wooden "crown" above the cab.

#### Turban (Dastar)

For Sikhs, the dastar is an article of faith, a turban that covers uncut hair (*kesh*). In Sikh tradition, the Gurus adopted it to declare the sovereignty and equality of all people.

**Script and sound:** ਦਸਤਾਰ, *dus-TAAR*

**Example:** Many Sikh men, and some Sikh women, tie a dastar each day to cover their uncut hair.

## U

#### Uganda Expulsion of 1972

The forced removal of nearly all South Asians from Uganda by the military ruler Idi Amin, who announced on 4 August 1972 that they had 90 days to leave.

**Example:** About 80,000 people of South Asian descent, many born in Uganda, had to abandon homes and businesses.

**See also:** [South Asians in East Africa](#south-asians-in-east-africa), [British South Asians](#british-south-asians)

#### Unani Medicine

A healing tradition that began with the Greek physicians Hippocrates and Galen, was developed by doctors writing in Arabic and Persian, and came to South Asia by the 1100s. It is based on the old idea of balancing four "humors."

**Script and sound:** یونانی, *yoo-NAA-nee*, "Greek"

**Example:** Unani practitioners are often called *hakims*.

**See also:** [Ayurveda](#ayurveda), [Indo-Persian Culture](#indo-persian-culture)

#### Unity in Diversity

The idea that a country can hold many languages, faiths, peoples and cultures and still be one nation, with differences seen as a strength. Indian leaders and schools have used the phrase since independence.

**Example:** An Indian school assembly with songs in several languages celebrates unity in diversity.

**See also:** [Regional Diversity](#regional-diversity), [Religious Pluralism](#religious-pluralism)

#### Urdu as National Language

The status of Urdu as the language Pakistan's constitution names for the whole nation, even though only a small share of Pakistanis speak it as a first language.

**Script and sound:** اردو, *OOR-doo*

**Example:** Article 251 of the 1973 constitution names Urdu the national language and lets provinces promote their own languages.

## V

#### Vaisakhi

A harvest festival and new year in Punjab and much of North India on 13 or 14 April, and one of the most important days of the Sikh year. It is also spelled Baisakhi.

**Script and sound:** *VAI-saa-khee*

**Example:** Sikhs mark Vaisakhi with prayers at the gurdwara and *nagar kirtan*, processions with hymn singing.

#### Varna and Jati

The two ideas behind what English speakers call caste. Varna is a fourfold social classification described in old Brahmanical Sanskrit texts; jati is the local group a person is born into, of which there are thousands.

**Script and sound:** वर्ण, *VAR-na* (varna); जाति, *JAA-tee*, "birth" or "kind" (jati)

**Example:** The *Manusmriti* lists four varnas and assigns each one duties.

**See also:** [Caste System](#caste-system)

*Taught in [Chapter 13: Family, Names, and Life Events](chapters/13-family-names-life-events/index.md).*

#### Vedic Period

The era from about 1500 to 500 BCE when the Vedas were composed in northern South Asia, between the end of the Indus cities and the rise of new cities and kingdoms around 600 BCE.

**Example:** The Rig Veda, the oldest of the Vedas, comes from the early part of the Vedic Period.

**See also:** [The Vedas](#the-vedas), [Sanskrit](#sanskrit)

*Taught in [Chapter 2: Ancient Roots](chapters/02-ancient-roots/index.md).*

#### Veena

An ancient name for stringed instruments used in Indian texts for about 3,000 years. In Carnatic music it usually means the Saraswati veena, a large plucked lute with 24 fixed frets.

**Script and sound:** वीणा, Tamil வீணை, *VEE-nah*

**Example:** The Hindu goddess Saraswati is usually shown holding a veena.

**See also:** [Carnatic Music](#carnatic-music)

#### Vegetarianism in South Asia

The practice of eating diets without meat, and often without fish or eggs, which is more common in South Asia than almost anywhere else, though most South Asians are not vegetarian.

**Example:** A Pew Research Center survey of nearly 30,000 adults in India, published in 2021, measured how many Indians are vegetarian.

#### Vesak

The most important Buddhist festival in South Asia, on the full moon of the month of Vesak, usually in May, when Buddhists remember the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and passing away. It is also called Buddha Purnima.

**Script and sound:** වෙසක්, *VAY-suck* (Sinhala)

#### Vijayanagara Empire

A large Hindu-ruled empire of southern India from 1336 to 1646, named after its capital city, Vijayanagara, "city of victory."

**Example:** The ruins of the capital city stand at Hampi, in Karnataka.

## W

#### Weddings

In South Asia, often multi-day events that join two families, not just two people, with separate ceremonies before, during and after the main rite. Details differ widely by faith, region and family budget.

**Example:** A wedding with a mehndi night, a nikah or other main ceremony, and a reception afterward can stretch across several days.

#### Western and Eastern Ghats

The two mountain ranges that frame the Deccan Plateau, one along each coast of peninsular India. *Ghat* means a flight of steps down to a river, or a mountain pass.

**Script and sound:** घाट, *GHAAT*

**Example:** The ranges act like giant steps from the plateau down to the sea on India's west and east coasts.

#### Wootz Steel

A high-carbon steel made in South India and Sri Lanka by melting iron with carbon-rich material in sealed clay crucibles, famous for its strength and wavy surface pattern. "Wootz" comes from *ukku*, the Kannada and Telugu word for steel.

**Example:** Finds in Tamil Nadu suggest crucible steel was made there before the Common Era.
