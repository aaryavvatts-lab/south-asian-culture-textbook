# How to Use This Book

## If you're a student

1. **Start anywhere.** Chapter 1 is the best first stop, but each chapter lists its prerequisites at the top, so you can jump to the festival or food chapter if that's what you're curious about.
2. **Play with the MicroSim.** Each chapter has one interactive simulation. Most take two minutes. Try the controls before you read the paragraph that explains them.
3. **Do the Ask Your Elders interview.** It's the most important part of the book. Ten minutes with a parent, grandparent or community elder will teach you things no book can.
4. **Check yourself.** Each chapter has five quick questions, twelve exercises and a separate 10-question quiz with explained answers.
5. **Use the glossary.** Every concept in the book has a short definition in the [Glossary](glossary.md).

## How words from other languages are shown

The first time a key word appears, you'll see it in its original script, a simple respelling and its meaning, like this: Diwali (दीवाली, *dee-VAH-lee*), "row of lamps". The CAPITAL letters show which part to stress. The respellings are approximate; the best way to learn a pronunciation is to ask someone who speaks the language. See [Appendix A](appendices/pronunciation-guide.md).

## Meet your guides

![Mora the peacock](img/mascot/neutral.png){ width="120" align="right" }

**Mora** is a young peacock who pops up in each chapter with a tip, a warning about a common mix-up, or a question to think about. The peacock motif runs through art across South Asia, from temple carvings to textiles.

**Zara Hossain and Ben Lindqvist** are two fictional sophomores at a Minnesota high school. Zara's family is from Bangladesh; Ben's is Swedish and Norwegian. They start a South Asian Student Association together, and each chapter begins with a question one of them can't answer yet. Their friends Arjun, Simran, Ayesha, Nima and Dilan show up along the way. All of them are made up.
