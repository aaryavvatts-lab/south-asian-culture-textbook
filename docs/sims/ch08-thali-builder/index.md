## About This MicroSim

A thali is a round plate that holds a whole meal: a staple grain in the middle and small bowls, called katori, around the edge. This MicroSim fills a thali for seven regions: Punjab (Pakistan and India), Bengal (Bangladesh and India), Kerala (India), Gujarat (India), Sindh (Pakistan), Sri Lanka and the hills of Nepal.

Each plate has the same seven slots: staple, dal, vegetable, main dish, side, sweet and main spices. Because the slots stay the same, you can switch regions and see what changes. A "Why here?" note explains how each region's grain, rain, coast or mountains, and trade history shape its food. A checkbox swaps the main dish between a common vegetarian dish and a common non-vegetarian dish from the same region, marked with a green or brown symbol.

Every plate is one common example. Real families in each region cook many more dishes, and no plate here is the "correct" one. No map or borders are drawn.

## How to Use

1. Start with the plate that loads (Bengal). Read the "Why here?" note.
2. Click or tap the center of the plate, then each bowl, and read what is in it.
3. Choose another region from the menu, or use **Previous** and **Next** to step through all seven.
4. Compare two regions slot by slot. Start with the staple in the center: is it rice or bread?
5. Tick **Non-vegetarian version** to see a common meat or fish dish in the main bowl. Notice which regions use fish and which use meat.
6. Look for ingredients that came from the Americas, like maize, potatoes, tomatoes and chili.

## Learning Objective

Students will be able to compare the everyday meals of different South Asian regions and explain how staple grains, climate, geography, faith and trade shape what each region eats.

**Bloom level:** Analyze (compare, connect causes to differences). Supports Understand (explain).

## Lesson Plan

**Grade level:** 9-12 (world geography, world history, ethnic studies, family and consumer sciences, or a South Asian student club meeting)

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Activity:**

1. (3 min) Warm-up: students write what they think "Indian food" or "South Asian food" is. Most will list a few restaurant dishes.
2. (7 min) In pairs, students fill a table with one row per region and columns for staple, dal, main dish and one spice.
3. (5 min) Each pair picks two regions and writes three sentences explaining one difference using the "Why here?" notes (grain, rain, coast, faith or trade).
4. (5 min) Class discussion: Which regions cross modern borders? Which ingredients came from the Americas? Why is the Gujarat plate usually vegetarian?

**Assessment questions:**

1. Which regions in the sim have rice as their staple, and which have bread? What do the rice regions have in common?
2. Name two ingredients on these plates that came from the Americas.
3. Why does the Bengal plate feature fish, while the Gujarat plate is usually vegetarian?
4. Which two regions in the sim lie in Pakistan, at least in part?
5. Design a thali for a region not in the sim, using the same seven slots.
